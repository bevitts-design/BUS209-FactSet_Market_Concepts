Weekly Business Operating Review

Turn business data into a focused update that explains what changed, why it matters, and what should happen next.

Evidence  •  Meaning  •  Action

What is an operating review?

An operating review is a concise, recurring update on performance. It combines a scorecard with context, risks, decisions, and action items.

Your goal: help the audience understand the current state of the business and leave knowing exactly what needs attention.

Viewing on a phone? Swipe tables left and right to see every column.

The seven-part review

1

Set the context

Name the business or team, the reporting period, and the people who prepared the review.
2

Summarize the week

Lead with the most important highlight, lowlight, and priority. Keep the summary short enough to read in under one minute.
3

Review the scorecard

Show a small set of meaningful metrics. Compare each result with a target, plan, prior period, or other useful benchmark.
4

Explain functional updates

For each business area, connect the result to its cause, business impact, and next step.
5

Map key milestones

Show the important dates, owners, and dependencies that affect delivery.
6

Surface risks and decisions

Identify what could disrupt the plan, the likely impact, and any decision or support the team needs.
7

Close with action

Record each action item with one owner, one due date, and a clear completion test.

Final quality check

☐ The opening states the business, time period, and purpose.

☐ Every important number has a comparison and an interpretation.

☐ Charts and tables are readable and support the main message.

☐ Risks, decisions, and routine updates are clearly separated.

☐ Each action has one owner and a due date.

☐ The audience can identify the top priority without guessing.

Keep it concise

If a detail does not explain performance, clarify a risk, support a decision, or define an action, move it to an appendix or leave it out.
